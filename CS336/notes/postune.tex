\section{Set Up}

Make some setup first

State S: prompt + generated response so far

Action A: generate next token

Rewards R: Deterministic Like the answer to a math question is right or not

Transition probabilities: $\mathbb{T}(s^{'} | s, a)$

Policy $\pi_{\theta}$: Language Model

Rollout: $s1 -> a1 -> s2 -> a2 -> ... -> s_{done} -> R$



\section{Policy Gradient}
The expection of reward is the integral over the s on reward given s, and policy action.
$$
\mathbb{E}[R] = \int \mathbb{P}(s)\pi_{\theta}(a|s) \mathbb{R}(s,a) \,d(s)
$$ 
Take gradient of it:
$$
\bigtriangledown_{\theta} \mathbb{E}[R] = \int  \mathbb{P}(s)\bigtriangledown_{\theta}\pi_{\theta}(a|s) \mathbb{R}(s,a) \,d(s) \\
$$
$$
\bigtriangledown_{\theta} \mathbb{E}[R] = \int  \mathbb{P}(s) \pi_{\theta}(a|s) \bigtriangledown_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(a|s) \mathbb{R}(s,a) \,d(s) \\
$$

$$
\bigtriangledown_{\theta} \mathbb{E}[R] = \mathbb{E} [ \bigtriangledown_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(a|s) \mathbb{R}(s,a) ]
$$

Sample prompt s, sample response from $\pi_{\theta}(a|s)$

Update paramters based on $\log \pi_{\theta}(a|s) \mathbb{R}(s,a)$

\section{DPO}

Start from the RLHF objective, max reward and penalty to a reference $\pi_{ref}$

$$
J(\pi) = \max_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{x,y\sim\pi(y|x)}[r(x,y)] - \beta D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot|x) || \pi_{ref}(\cdot|x))
$$

Our goal is to find the policy $\pi_{r}$ that maximizes this $J(\pi)$
\subsection{Step 1: Expand the KL divergence}

$$
D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot|x) || \pi_{ref}(\cdot|x)) = \sum_{y}\pi(y|x)[\log\pi(y|x) - \log\pi_{ref}(y|x)]
$$

Substitute that back into $J_{\pi}$:

$$
J(\pi) = \sum_{y}\pi(y|x)[r(x,y)] - \beta\sum_{y}\pi(y|x)[\log\pi(y|x) - \log\pi_{ref}(y|x)]
$$

simplify:

$$
J(\pi) = \sum_{y}\pi(y|x)[r(x,y) - \beta\log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)} ]
$$

We must also keep in mind that probabilities sum to 1:
$$
\sum_{y}\pi(y|x) = 1
$$

\subsection{Step 2: Optimize over all possible $\pi$}

We now want to find the probability distribution $\pi(y|x)$ that maximizes $J(\pi)$
That’s a constrained optimization problem with the normalization constraint.
We use a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda(x)$ for the constraint and form:

$$
\mathcal{L}(\pi, \lambda) = \sum_{y}\pi(y|x)[r(x,y) - \beta\log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)} ] + \lambda(x)[1 - \sum_{y}\pi(y|x)]
$$

\subsection{Step 3: Take derivative and set to zero}

Take derivative of $\mathcal{L}$ w.r.t. $\pi(y|x)$ and set to 0:
$$
    \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \pi(y|x)} = r(x,y) - \beta[\log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)} + 1] - \lambda(x) = 0
$$

solve for $\log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)}$:

$$
\log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)} =  \frac{1}{\beta}[r(x,y) - \lambda(x)] - 1
$$

Exponentiate both sides:

$$
\pi(y|x) = \pi_{ref}(y|x)\exp({\frac{1}{\beta}[r(x,y) - \lambda(x)] - 1})
$$

The constants $1$ and $\lambda(x)$ 
can be absorbed into a single normalization constant $Z(x)$, because they do not depend on $y$

$$
\pi(y|x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)} \pi_{ref}(y|x)\exp({\frac{1}{\beta}[r(x,y)]})
$$
where $Z(x)$ ensures the probabilities sum to 1 (for Lagrange).

$$
\pi(y|x) \propto \pi_{ref}(y|x)\exp({\frac{1}{\beta}[r(x,y)]})
$$

Intuition:

High-reward actions get exponentially higher probability.

But it’s still shaped by the reference model’s probabilities.

$\beta$ acts like a temperature: small $\beta$ → more concentrated on high rewards.

\subsection{Invert the relation to express reward in terms of the policy}

From that equation, take log and rearrange:
$$
    r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)} + \beta \log Z(x)
$$

where $Z(x)$ is a normalization constant to make probabilities sum to 1.

\subsection{Bring in preference data}
Human preference data says: on the same prompt $x$, completion $y_w$ (winner) is preferred over $y_{l}$ (loser).

$$
P(y_w > y_l | x) = \sigma(r(y_w, x) - r(y_l, x))
$$

Now plug in our expression for $r(x,y)$ from Step 3:
$$
    r(y_w, x) - r(y_l, x) = \beta [ \log \frac{\pi(y_w|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w|x)} - \log \frac{\pi(y_l|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l|x)} ]
$$

and you want to maximizes the p:

$$
    \mathcal{L}_{DPO} = -\log \sigma (\beta [ \log \frac{\pi(y_w|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w|x)} - \log \frac{\pi(y_l|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l|x)} ]
)
$$
